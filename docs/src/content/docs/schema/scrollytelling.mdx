---
title: Writing a story file
description: How a story file is put together — the map, the chapters, camera moves, layers and actions — and how a story map behaves as the reader scrolls.
---

A story file is one YAML document, `type: scrollytelling`, that describes a
story map: a map, and the chapters that scroll over it. Each chapter moves the
camera, and can show or hide layers and run actions (this is often called
*scrollytelling*). This page explains how to write one. To put it on a page,
see [Story maps in plain HTML](/integrations/scrollytelling-html/) or
[Story maps in Astro](/integrations/astro-stories/); every key, with its type
and default, is in the generated
[YAML reference for stories](/reference/yaml/scrollytelling/).

## Quick start

A story needs a `type`, an `id`, the map's `config:` and at least one chapter.
This one draws the past week's earthquakes from the USGS feed and visits two
places:

```yaml title="story.yaml"
type: scrollytelling
id: quakes-this-week

# The map when the page opens: its basemap and camera.
config:
  mapStyle: "https://tiles.openfreemap.org/styles/liberty"
  center: [0, 20]
  zoom: 1.5

# Layers drawn for the whole story.
layers:
  - id: quakes
    type: circle
    source:
      type: geojson
      url: "https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/feed/v1.0/summary/4.5_week.geojson"
    paint:
      circle-radius: 5
      circle-color: "#dc2626"

chapters:
  - id: world
    title: "A week of earthquakes"
    description: "<p>Every earthquake of magnitude 4.5 or more in the past seven days.</p>"
    center: [0, 20]
    zoom: 1.5
  - id: japan
    title: "Japan"
    description: "<p>Four plates meet under Japan.</p>"
    center: [138, 37]
    zoom: 4.5
```

Show it with `<ml-scrollytelling src="story.yaml">` or the Astro
`<Scrollytelling>` component. The map opens on the whole world; scroll and it
flies to Japan as the second card reaches the middle of the screen.

- `id` names the story. Keep it unique on the page.
- `config:` is the map's starting point, as in any
  [map document](/getting-started/first-map/): `mapStyle` (the basemap),
  `center` and `zoom`. Its other keys (`maxZoom`, `locale`, gestures) work too.
- `layers:` are drawn for the whole story. Chapters show, hide and restyle
  them.
- Each chapter needs an `id`, a `title`, a `center` and a `zoom`.

Check a story without a browser with `npx maplibre-yaml validate story.yaml`.
Each problem is listed with its line number and, for a misspelt key, a
did-you-mean (`centre` → `center`). In the browser, the same list shows in
place of the story.

## Common tasks

### Write a chapter's text, pictures and video \{#media-in-chapters}

- `description` is HTML, shown as written: `<p>`, `<strong>`,
  `<a href="…">`. Markdown is not converted, so `**bold**` shows literally.
- `image:` and `video:` show above the text. Give a full `https://` URL, or
  the path of a file on your site: on an Astro or Vite site, a file in
  `public/images/fault.jpg` is `/images/fault.jpg`. Images load only as the
  reader reaches them, and are never taller than 40% of the screen.

```yaml
chapters:
  - id: fuji
    title: "Mount Fuji"
    description: "<p>Last erupted in <strong>1707</strong>.</p>"
    image: "/images/fuji.jpg"
    center: [138.73, 35.36]
    zoom: 9
```

A chapter with `hidden: true` has no card: the map moves, and the reader sees
nothing but the map until the next chapter.

### Place the card \{#content-alignment}

`alignment:` puts the card on the `left`, on the `right`, in the `center`
(the default), or across the `full` width. The camera is offset so the
chapter's `center` isn't hidden behind its card: beside a left or right card,
above a centred or full-width one. On phones every card sits in the lower part
of the screen, and the chapter's `center` shows above it.

```yaml
chapters:
  - id: intro
    title: "Introduction"
    center: [0, 20]
    zoom: 1.5
    alignment: full
  - id: andes
    title: "The Andes"
    center: [-70, -20]
    zoom: 4
    alignment: left
```

`cameraOffset: false`, at the top of the file, turns the offset off: each
`center` is then the middle of the whole map. A chapter's own `padding:` (in
pixels, one number or `{ top, right, bottom, left }`) replaces the offset for
that chapter.

### Move the camera \{#animation-types}

Each chapter sets the camera with `center` and `zoom`, and optionally `pitch`
(tilt, 0–85) and `bearing` (rotation, −180 to 180). `animation:` says how the
map gets there:

- `flyTo` (the default) zooms out, travels and zooms back in, in an arc.
  `speed` (default 0.6) and `curve` (default 1) tune the arc.
- `easeTo` moves straight there, with no zoom-out arc. Use it between nearby
  places.
- `jumpTo` cuts there at once.

```yaml
chapters:
  - id: tokyo
    title: "Tokyo from above"
    center: [139.69, 35.69]
    zoom: 12
    pitch: 60
    bearing: -30
    animation: easeTo
```

While a chapter is active, `rotateAnimation: true` slowly turns the camera,
and `spinGlobe: true` slowly pans it westward (for a globe, set
`projection: { type: globe }` at the top of the file). Both stop at the next
chapter. When the reader's system asks for reduced motion, every camera move
is instant and neither runs.

### Show and hide layers \{#layer-control}

`layers: { show: [...], hide: [...] }` on a chapter changes which layers are
visible, by layer `id`. The names can also be the basemap's own layers.

```yaml
chapters:
  - id: plates
    title: "Plate boundaries"
    center: [140, 20]
    zoom: 3
    layers:
      show: [plate-boundaries]
      hide: [quakes]
```

A chapter's changes stay until another chapter undoes them. Give each layer
its starting state in the layer itself: a layer that a later chapter reveals
starts with `layout: { visibility: none }`. A name that is a near miss of one
of the story's layers (`quake` for `quakes`) gets a did-you-mean warning; a
name unlike any of them is taken to be a basemap layer.

### Run actions as a chapter starts or ends \{#chapter-actions}

`onChapterEnter` runs when a chapter becomes active, after its camera move and
layer changes. `onChapterExit` runs when the reader scrolls past it, before
the next chapter's camera move. Each is a list of actions:

| `action` | What it does | Its fields |
|---|---|---|
| `setFilter` | Filter a layer's features | `layer`, `filter` (an expression, or `null` to clear it) |
| `setPaintProperty` | Change a paint property | `layer`, `property`, `value` |
| `setLayoutProperty` | Change a layout property | `layer`, `property`, `value` |
| `flyTo` | Fly the camera | `options`: `center`, `zoom`, `pitch`, `bearing`, `duration`, … |
| `easeTo` | Ease the camera, with no arc | `options`, as for `flyTo` |
| `fitBounds` | Frame an area | `bounds`: `[west, south, east, north]` or `[[west, south], [east, north]]`; `options`: `padding`, `duration`, … |
| `custom` | Run nothing itself; tells your page script | `options`: anything your script reads |

```yaml
chapters:
  - id: strongest
    title: "The strongest"
    center: [0, 20]
    zoom: 1.5
    onChapterEnter:
      - action: setFilter
        layer: quakes
        filter: [">=", ["get", "mag"], 6]
      - action: setPaintProperty
        layer: quakes
        property: circle-radius
        value: 10
    onChapterExit:
      - action: setFilter
        layer: quakes
        filter: null
      - action: setPaintProperty
        layer: quakes
        property: circle-radius
        value: 5
```

An action that names a layer the map doesn't have does nothing, and the map's
notice says so while you develop. Under reduced motion the camera actions are
instant, unless their `options` set `essential: true`.

### Run your own code from a chapter \{#custom-actions}

A `custom` action runs nothing itself. The story element dispatches an
`ml-scrollytelling:action` event with the action, so a script on the page can
do anything with it:

```yaml
chapters:
  - id: deep
    title: "Deep earthquakes"
    center: [-70, -20]
    zoom: 4
    callback: deep-quakes
    onChapterEnter:
      - action: custom
        options: { banner: "Deeper than 300 km" }
```

```js
const banner = document.getElementById("banner");
document.addEventListener("ml-scrollytelling:action", (event) => {
  const { action, phase } = event.detail; // also: chapterId, and map (the MapLibre map)
  if (phase === "enter") banner.textContent = action.options.banner;
});
```

`callback:` is a name you choose. It does nothing by itself: it arrives as
`callback` on the `ml-scrollytelling:chapter` event, which fires on every
chapter change. Both events are in the
[`<ml-scrollytelling>` reference](/reference/elements/#ml-scrollytelling-events).

### Add the chapter rail \{#chapter-navigation-rail}

`showMarkers: true` adds a column of dots at the right edge of the map, one
per chapter. Despite the key's name, nothing is drawn on the map itself; to
mark chapter places on the map, add a layer. Readers click a dot to jump to
its chapter, or Tab to it and use the arrow keys, Home and End. The current
chapter's dot is larger and filled, so it stands out by more than colour.
`markerColor` fills the dots with a colour of your own.

```yaml no-validate
showMarkers: true
markerColor: "#f97316"
```

### The legend

A story's legend is pinned: it stays in one corner, above the chapter cards,
while the story scrolls. Write it as on a map, with `title` and `items`:

```yaml no-validate
legend:
  title: Earthquakes
  collapsed: auto        # auto (the default), true or false
  # position: top-right  # optional; the default follows the cards
  items:
    - { color: "#dc2626", label: "Magnitude 4.5 or more", shape: circle }
```

- **Corner.** `position:` if you set one. Otherwise top-left (away from the
  chapter rail), or top-right on a wide screen whose cards are all
  `alignment: left`.
- **Open, or a button.** With `collapsed: auto` the legend is a "Legend"
  button on phones, and on wide screens where cards pass its corner (cards on
  both sides, or full-width). It is open everywhere else. `true` always starts
  it as a button, `false` always open. The button's word is translated by
  `maplibre-yaml.Legend.Title` in `config.locale`.
- **The panel.** The button opens the legend above the cards. The page stays
  usable; Escape or (on a phone) a tap outside closes it.

A story with a [`classify:`](/guides/thematic-maps/) layer, or a layer with
its own `legend:` entry, gets a legend without a `legend:` block, by the same
rules. `legend: false` at the top turns it off. A `slot="legend"` child of the
story element fills the panel with your own markup instead.

### Use the rest of a map document \{#map-level-keys}

A story's map is an ordinary map document, so the top-level keys a
[`type: map` document](/schema/map-config/) takes work here too, with the same
rules: `title:` and `description:` (the map's name and summary for screen
readers), named `sources:`, standalone `markers:` and `popups:`,
`controls:`, `images:`, the params panel (`state:` and `parameters:`), and
`terrain:`, `sky:`, `light:` and `projection:`. Write them at the top of the
story file, not under `config:`.

```yaml
type: scrollytelling
id: harbour-tour
title: "New York harbour"
config:
  mapStyle: "https://tiles.openfreemap.org/styles/liberty"
  center: [-74.02, 40.69]
  zoom: 12
sources:
  piers:
    type: geojson
    locations:
      - { coordinates: [-74.017, 40.703], name: Battery Park }
      - { coordinates: [-73.997, 40.705], name: South Street Seaport }
layers:
  - id: pier-dots
    type: circle
    source: piers
    paint:
      circle-radius: 8
      circle-color: "#0ea5e9"
markers:
  - at: [-74.0445, 40.6892]
    color: "#16a34a"
    popup:
      - h3:
          - str: Statue of Liberty
chapters:
  - id: piers
    title: "The piers"
    center: [-74.01, 40.70]
    zoom: 13
  - id: liberty
    title: "Liberty Island"
    center: [-74.0445, 40.6892]
    zoom: 14
```

The story map's own pan and zoom gestures start off, so the reader's wheel
and finger scroll the story. Markers, popups, the legend and the params panel
stay clickable. To let readers move the map, turn a gesture back on in
`config:` (`scrollZoom: true`, `dragPan: true`).

### Theme, footer and the container \{#themes}

- `theme: dark` gives the cards light text on a dark background; `light` is
  the default.
- `footer:` is HTML shown after the last chapter.
- `className:` adds classes to the story element, and `style:` adds inline
  CSS to it, for example `style: "--ml-scrolly-map-height: 80vh"`. The class
  names and custom properties you can style are in
  [Styling](/integrations/scrollytelling-html/#styling).

```yaml no-validate
theme: dark
className: quake-story
footer: "<p>Data: <a href=\"https://earthquake.usgs.gov/\">USGS</a></p>"
```

## How a story behaves

- **The active chapter** is the one whose card is on screen and nearest the
  middle of it, recomputed as the page scrolls. The story starts reacting once
  its first chapter reaches the middle 20% of the screen. On phones the map
  moves on once the next card is on screen.
- **On load**, the map opens on the `config:` camera, then at once applies the
  chapter in that middle zone. On a page that starts with the story, the first
  chapter's camera, `layers` and `onChapterEnter` actions all run.
- **Changing chapter** runs the previous chapter's `onChapterExit` actions,
  moves the camera, applies `layers`, then runs `onChapterEnter`.
- **Reading comes first.** The wheel and touch scroll the story, not the map.
  The rail, legend, markers, popups and slots stay clickable. Nothing on the
  map moves with the scroll: the legend, controls and corner slots stay in
  their corners, under the cards.
- **Reduced motion.** Cards stop animating, every camera move and camera
  action is instant (an action whose `options` set `essential: true` still
  animates), and `rotateAnimation` and `spinGlobe` are off.
- **Problems.** A story that fails to load or validate shows one card listing
  each problem with its line number, instead of the story. A story map that is
  slow to start shows "Loading map…" until its basemap draws.

## A complete example \{#complete-examples}

The past week's earthquakes, coloured by magnitude, with the chapter rail, a
legend and a chapter that filters to the strongest:

```yaml title="story.yaml"
type: scrollytelling
id: earthquake-story
title: "A week of earthquakes"
theme: dark
showMarkers: true
markerColor: "#f97316"
config:
  mapStyle: "https://tiles.openfreemap.org/styles/liberty"
  center: [0, 20]
  zoom: 1.5
legend:
  title: Magnitude
  items:
    - { color: "#fbbf24", label: "4.5 to 6", shape: circle }
    - { color: "#dc2626", label: "6 or more", shape: circle }
layers:
  - id: quakes
    type: circle
    source:
      type: geojson
      url: "https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/feed/v1.0/summary/4.5_week.geojson"
    paint:
      circle-radius: ["interpolate", ["linear"], ["get", "mag"], 4.5, 4, 8, 16]
      circle-color: ["step", ["get", "mag"], "#fbbf24", 6, "#dc2626"]
      circle-stroke-width: 1
      circle-stroke-color: "#ffffff"
chapters:
  - id: world
    title: "A week of earthquakes"
    description: "<p>Every earthquake of magnitude 4.5 or more in the past seven days.</p>"
    center: [0, 20]
    zoom: 1.5
    alignment: full
  - id: ring-of-fire
    title: "The Ring of Fire"
    description: "<p>Most of them follow the edges of the Pacific.</p>"
    center: [160, 10]
    zoom: 2
    alignment: left
  - id: japan
    title: "Japan"
    description: "<p>Four plates meet under Japan.</p>"
    center: [138, 37]
    zoom: 4.5
    pitch: 40
    alignment: right
  - id: strongest
    title: "The strongest"
    description: "<p>Only magnitude 6 and above.</p>"
    center: [0, 20]
    zoom: 1.5
    onChapterEnter:
      - action: setFilter
        layer: quakes
        filter: [">=", ["get", "mag"], 6]
    onChapterExit:
      - action: setFilter
        layer: quakes
        filter: null
footer: "<p>Data: USGS earthquake feed, updated every minute.</p>"
```

The repository's
[`examples/scrollytelling`](https://github.com/design-practices/maplibre-yaml/tree/main/examples/scrollytelling)
story uses every kind of action, on a plain HTML page.

## Troubleshooting

Problems every map shares are in [Troubleshooting](/troubleshooting/).

- **`**bold**` shows literally in a chapter.** `description` is HTML; write
  `<strong>bold</strong>`.
- **"Two chapters have id …".** Each chapter needs its own `id`; the message
  names both lines. Copying a chapter and keeping its `id` is the usual cause.
- **A layer doesn't appear or disappear.** Check the chapter's `layers` ids
  against the layers' `id`s. A repeated layer `id` warns, and only the first
  layer with it is drawn.
- **A key under `config:` does nothing**, such as `legend:` or `sources:`.
  These go at the top of the story file; the map's notice says so while you
  develop.
- **The map scrolls away with the chapters.** A wrapper around the story has
  `overflow: hidden`, `auto` or `scroll`, which stops `position: sticky`.
