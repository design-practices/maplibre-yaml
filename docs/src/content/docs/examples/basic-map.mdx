The smallest map with data of your own: five US cities written into the
document, drawn as circles sized by population, with a popup when you click
one. Read it after the [quick start](/getting-started/quick-start/), when
you want to see a layer, its data and its styling together.

<ExampleMap config="basic-map.yaml" height="460px" caption="Click a city for its population" />

## How it works

**The data is part of the document.** The layer's `source:` is GeoJSON
written under `data:`, a `FeatureCollection` with one point per city, so the
map needs no other file. For data in a file of its own, give the source a
`url:` instead ([Loading data](/guides/data-sources/)).

**`bounds:` frames the cities.** The camera opens on that box (west, south,
east, north) whatever the map's size, so all five cities show on a phone as
well as a wide screen. `center:` and `zoom:` are still required.

**The circle size follows population.** `interpolate` with `["linear"]`
scales the radius between the stops you list, by the value of
`["get", "population"]`:

<YamlExcerpt config="basic-map.yaml" at={["circle-radius:"]} />

A city of 2.7 million (Chicago) falls between the first two stops and gets
about 14 px. These are MapLibre
[expressions](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/expressions/), written
as YAML lists; [Layers & styling](/guides/layers/) explains them.

**A click opens a popup.** The `interactive:` block names what the popup
shows, and `format:` writes the population with separators, so New York
reads 8,336,817:
